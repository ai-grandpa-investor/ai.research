Same AI, same facts, 24% apart: what two research runs said about Intel's earnings

Intel (INTC)

Hypothesis rejected

The short version

We asked the same AI the same questions about Intel twice more after a first answer, once with standard thinking and once with deep thinking. Both times it found the same facts, and caught the same two mistakes in our own notes. Yet its forecasts for Intel's 2028 earnings, weighted by the odds it gave each scenario, ended 24% apart.

The bear cases matched, at about $1.57 a share. What differed most was how likely the AI thought each scenario was. With deep thinking it put 62% on the two gloomiest outcomes; with standard thinking, 21%. Give each rerun the other's odds and their answers swap places: $3.24 and $1.98. It also disagreed with itself on how many future shares to count (stock awards, and shares held in escrow that may yet be issued).

Why it matters

Checking an AI's facts is worth doing, and it is cheap to automate. But it fixes the part that was already right. The number that moves a valuation most here is a probability, and there is no document to check a probability against.

What we changed

We had planned an automatic loop: research, fact-check, ask again about what is still open, repeat. We will not build it. One research pass and a fact-check are enough for the facts. The odds of each scenario are set by a person, or read from what the market price implies, and the AI is asked for the scenarios, not for their odds.

How it was run

We asked an AI (Claude Opus 5.5) ten questions about Intel's earnings in fiscal 2028, with web search. It answered with scenarios, each with revenue, profit margin, share count and odds, and our code checked that each scenario's earnings add up. The first answer came from a chat, with deep thinking. We then fixed the gaps in the questions and asked twice more in automated runs, once with standard and once with deep thinking. Every fact and figure in the answers went into a log and was checked wherever we could.

Question
If we fact-check an AI's research on Intel and ask it again, does its earnings forecast get better?
Hypothesis
A second, fact-checked pass corrects the first answer: some scenario's earnings move by more than 5%, or a fact the first answer stated turns out wrong.

What changed from the earlier run:

  • Questions no public source can answer were dropped.
  • We named the past cases to compare valuations with, across more than one market cycle.
  • A scenario with demand falling was required, with the reasoning behind every probability.
  • Every number had to come with its link.

The test set in advance: partly met

Before running, we decided to build an automatic research-and-fact-check loop only if the second pass moved some scenario's earnings by more than 5%, or showed that a fact in the first answer was wrong.

  • MetA scenario's earnings moved by more than 5%. They did, but in opposite directions: the standard rerun cut the base case by 7.6%, the deep rerun raised it by 13.7%. That is two runs disagreeing, not a correction.
  • Could not be scoredA fact in the first answer turned out wrong. The first answer's figures had not been saved one by one, so they could not be tested. Both reruns disputed one of its statements: that a risk warning about Intel's next manufacturing process had been dropped from its filings.
